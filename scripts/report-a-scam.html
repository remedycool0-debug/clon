1. Take immediate action

  1. Stop sending money. Pause contact with the suspected scammer. Do not pay more to unlock a withdrawal or release supposed earnings.
  2. Contact your payment provider immediately. Use the number on your card or the provider's official website. Explain whether you authorized the payment or someone used your account without permission. Ask whether the transaction can be stopped, recalled, disputed, or refunded.
  3. Secure affected accounts. Change compromised passwords from a trusted device, enable multifactor authentication, and remove unfamiliar access. If someone accessed your device remotely, follow the FTC's device-security guidance.

For steps specific to cards, bank wires, payment apps, gift cards, and cryptocurrency, see the FTC's guide to what to do after a scam.

2. Gather your evidence

Keep original records and make a short timeline. Include the information you already have; you do not need every item before reporting.

  • Names, business names, usernames, phone numbers, email addresses, and website addresses used by the scammer.
  • Dates, amounts, payment methods, receipts, statements, and transaction reference numbers.
  • Emails, messages, screenshots, advertisements, contracts, and promises about returns or withdrawals.
  • For cryptocurrency: the exchange used, blockchain network, wallet addresses, and transaction hashes.
  • A description of what happened and any report or case reference numbers.

Store evidence securely. Send it through the receiving agency's official process; never share passwords, one-time codes, or wallet recovery phrases.

4. Understand asset recovery

Asset recovery means seeking the return of money or property lost through fraud. The available route depends on the payment, the people involved, and whether assets can be located.

Recovery is not guaranteed. You may recover only part of a loss, and proceedings can take a long time. Filing a complaint does not automatically open a refund claim.

Payment disputes and recalls

Your bank or payment provider can explain available processes and deadlines. Act promptly. Cryptocurrency transfers are typically irreversible; contact the exchange or provider anyway to report the transaction.

Official distributions and court proceedings

Some securities cases result in SEC Fair Funds, disgorgement distributions, or court-appointed receiverships. Eligibility and claim deadlines are case-specific. Verify any administrator and claim instructions through the regulator or court's own website.

Arbitration, mediation, and civil claims

A dispute involving a brokerage firm may qualify for arbitration or mediation. A licensed attorney or securities arbitration clinic can explain whether a private claim is appropriate and which deadlines apply.

Read the FINRA guide to legitimate recovery options and Investor.gov's explanation of recovery processes.

5. Avoid a second scam

People who have lost money may be targeted again by someone promising to recover it. Be cautious about unsolicited offers, guaranteed results, and demands for an upfront tax, processing fee, or cryptocurrency payment to release funds.

Independently verify who is contacting you using an official website. A logo, case number, or document alone does not prove authority. Never give a supposed recovery agent access to your account or wallet.

Common questions

Will reporting guarantee that I get my money back?

No. Reports can help agencies identify fraud and investigate. A refund or distribution depends on the circumstances, available assets, and any applicable claims process.

What should I do after filing?

Save your submission and reference number. Keep new evidence and follow the agency's instructions for updates. Continue working with your bank on any dispute and check relevant claim deadlines separately.

Can stolen cryptocurrency be recovered?

Yes, recovery is possible. If you need to track a transaction you wish to recover, please contact us via our chat to inquire about the available recovery options.

Can I file a complaint on this page or through the chat?

No. This page provides information and links. Submit your complaint directly through the official reporting websites above. Do not send financial documents or account credentials through this site's chat.
